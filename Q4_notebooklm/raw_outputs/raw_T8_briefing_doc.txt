Comprehensive Briefing: ASIC Regulatory Guide 280 on Mandatory Sustainability ReportingExecutive SummaryIn March 2025, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) issued Regulatory Guide 280 (Sustainability reporting) (RG 280). RG 280 provides comprehensive administrative guidance on Australia’s statutory mandatory sustainability reporting framework established under Chapter 2M of the Corporations Act 2001 (Corporations Act).The primary objective of the mandatory sustainability reporting regime is to improve the quality, consistency, and comparability of climate-related financial disclosures. This regime aims to enable investors, lenders, creditors, and other market participants to make confident, informed resource allocation decisions while assisting financial regulators in monitoring systemic climate risks across the economy.Key takeaways from ASIC Regulatory Guide 280 include:Scope and Phase-In Timelines: Sustainability reporting is phased across three entity groups starting between 1 January 2025 and 1 July 2027 based on consolidated financial size, National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting Act 2007 (NGER Act) status, or total asset metrics for asset-owner entities (registrable superannuation entities [RSEs], registered schemes, and retail corporate collective investment vehicles [CCIVs]).Core Disclosures: Under Australian Accounting Standards Board standard AASB S2 (Climate-related disclosures), covered entities must disclose climate-related financial risks and opportunities across governance, strategy, risk management, metrics, and targets. This includes mandated climate scenario analysis using specific temperature benchmarks and absolute gross Scope 3 greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions (including financed emissions).Group 3 Relief Provision: Entities in the smallest reporting cohort (Group 3) that determine they have no material climate-related financial risks or opportunities may lodge a simplified statement explaining their determination under section 296B(1) of the Corporations Act.Governance and Director Obligations: Directors owe duties of care and diligence regarding sustainability reporting. For financial years commencing between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2027, a modified directors' declaration applies, requiring directors to state that the entity took "reasonable steps" to ensure compliance. A full opinion-based declaration takes effect for reporting periods starting on or after 1 January 2028.Modified Liability Regime: Temporary statutory immunity from private civil litigation applies to "protected statements" (such as Scope 3 emissions, scenario analysis, transition plans, and forward-looking climate statements made during early reporting years). Only criminal proceedings or regulatory actions brought by ASIC are permitted for these statements.Record Keeping: Reporting entities must retain written "sustainability records" explaining the methods, assumptions, and evidence supporting their reports for seven years. Failure to maintain these records constitutes a strict liability offence.ASIC Oversight and Enforcement: ASIC will adopt a proportionate, pragmatic supervisory approach during the transition. However, ASIC possesses sweeping directions powers under section 296E to require correction, substantiation, or publication of statements in sustainability reports, and will enforce strict liability for failure to lodge or serious/reckless misconduct.1. Statutory Framework and ASIC Regulatory ToolkitStatutory BasisChapter 2M of the Corporations Act mandates that entities meeting specified statutory criteria must prepare, lodge, and disclose an annual sustainability report alongside their financial report, directors' report, and auditor's report.Under section 224 of the Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001 (ASIC Act), the sustainability reporting architecture aims to deliver relevant, reliable, and comparable financial disclosures that lower the cost of capital, strengthen international competitiveness, and facilitate efficient capital allocation.ASIC Regulatory Document ClassificationASIC administers legislation using four distinct types of regulatory documents, as defined in RG 280 and illustrated in ASIC framework documentation:Document TypePrimary Purpose and Regulatory FunctionConsultation PapersSeek stakeholder feedback on matters ASIC is considering, such as proposed class relief or regulatory guidance.Regulatory GuidesExplain when and how ASIC exercises specific statutory powers, detail ASIC's statutory interpretations, outline underlying policy principles, and offer practical compliance guidance (e.g., application steps or compliance examples).Information SheetsProvide concise guidance on specific compliance issues or processes, or provide an overview of complex, detailed guidance.ReportsDescribe ASIC compliance or relief activity, or publish the findings of ASIC research projects.2. Scope, Entity Coverage, and Phasing ScheduleCovered EntitiesEntities required to prepare an annual financial report under Chapter 2M (referred to as "Chapter 2M entities") must prepare an annual sustainability report if they satisfy any of the statutory thresholds under section 292A. These entities include:Companies (including corporations registered under the NGER Act);Registered managed investment schemes;Registrable superannuation entities (RSEs); andRetail corporate collective investment vehicles (CCIVs).Note on Responsibilities: For registered schemes, the responsible entity performs the reporting obligations (section 285(3)). For RSEs, the RSE licensee performs the obligations (sections 285(3A) and 345AAA). For retail CCIVs, reporting is conducted on behalf of each relevant sub-fund (section 1232C(1)(aa)).Phasing Thresholds (Section 1707B)Sustainability reporting obligations are phased across three distinct entity cohorts based on metrics evaluated at the conclusion of each financial year:Reporting CohortAnnual Reporting Period CommencementApplicable Statutory Thresholds (Meet at least 2 of 3 Corporate Criteria, or Specific Asset/Emissions Thresholds)Group 11 January 2025 to 30 June 2026Corporate Size Criteria:<br>• Consolidated revenue $\ge \$500$ million;<br>• Consolidated gross assets $\ge \$1$ billion; or<br>• Employees $\ge 500$.<br>NGER Threshold: Registered corporations under NGER Act (or required to apply under s12(1)) meeting the group threshold in s13(1)(a) of the NGER Act.Group 21 July 2026 to 30 June 2027Corporate Size Criteria:<br>• Consolidated revenue $\ge \$200$ million;<br>• Consolidated gross assets $\ge \$500$ million; or<br>• Employees $\ge 250$.<br>NGER Threshold: All registered corporations under NGER Act (or required to apply under s12(1)).<br>Asset-Owner Threshold: Registered schemes, RSEs, and retail CCIVs with consolidated asset values $\ge \$5$ billion at financial year-end. (For retail CCIVs, the $\$5$ billion threshold applies to the entire CCIV across all sub-funds).Group 31 July 2027 onwardsCorporate Size Criteria:<br>• Consolidated revenue $\ge \$50$ million;<br>• Consolidated gross assets $\ge \$25$ million; or<br>• Employees $\ge 100$.<br>(Includes registered schemes, RSEs, and retail CCIVs meeting these corporate size criteria).Technical Rules for Threshold DeterminationCrystallisation at Year-End: Statutory thresholds crystallize at the end of the financial year. Entities must maintain tracking systems to monitor potential changes in reporting status resulting from corporate acquisitions, restructures, or shifting economic conditions during the year.Control and Consolidation: Consolidated entities determine control in accordance with Australian Accounting Standard AASB 10 (Consolidated Financial Statements) pursuant to section 292A(7)(a).Revenue and Asset Calculation: Revenue is determined per AASB 15 (Revenue from Contracts with Customers) and related standards (e.g., AASB 9, AASB 11, AASB 17, AASB 140, AASB 141). Total assets reflect values reported in standalone or consolidated balance sheets. Employee counts refer to definition concepts under AASB 2 (Share-based Payment) aligning with section 45A(3) of the Corporations Act.3. Exemptions and Non-Reporting EntitiesEntities not required to prepare a Chapter 2M sustainability report include:Foreign companies registered under Division 2 of Part 5B.2 and entities incorporated in foreign jurisdictions;Small proprietary companies with no Chapter 2M annual financial reporting duties;Registered charities regulated exclusively under the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission Act 2012; andEntities that have obtained ASIC relief from preparing an annual financial report under Chapter 2M for the relevant financial year.Consolidated and Stapled Group Reporting ProvisionsAustralian Consolidated Groups: A subsidiary is exempt from preparing an individual sustainability report if its Australian parent entity prepares a consolidated sustainability report under section 292A(2) and is required by accounting standards to prepare consolidated financial statements for Chapter 2M.Foreign Parent Entities: A foreign parent entity is not a Chapter 2M entity and cannot prepare a consolidated sustainability report under section 292A(2). Consequently, Australian subsidiaries of foreign parents that meet the reporting thresholds must prepare individual sustainability reports unless specific relief is granted by ASIC.Stapled Entities: Under ASIC Corporations (Reporting by Stapled Entities) Instrument 2023/673, a stapled entity can elect to prepare a single consolidated or combined sustainability report on behalf of all members of the stapled group as if they were a single entity.4. Components and Technical Disclosures of the Sustainability ReportUnder section 296A, a complete annual sustainability report comprises:Climate statements;Notes to climate statements (if required); andThe directors' declaration.Core Disclosure Content (AASB S2)Unless a section 296B(1) exemption applies, climate statements must comply with section 296D and standard AASB S2 (which aligns with IFRS S2 developed by the International Sustainability Standards Board [ISSB]). Disclosures cover four core pillars:Governance: The governance processes, controls, and procedures used to monitor and manage climate-related risks and opportunities.Strategy: The strategy for addressing climate-related risks and opportunities that could affect the entity's business model, value chain, and financial prospects over the short, medium, and long term.Risk Management: The processes used to identify, assess, prioritize, and monitor climate-related risks and opportunities.Metrics and Targets: Performance against cross-industry climate metrics, internal carbon prices, remuneration linkages, and quantitative/qualitative targets.       ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

Exemption for Group 3 Entities: Section 296B(1) StatementA Group 3 reporting entity that assesses that it has no material financial risks or opportunities relating to climate for a financial year is exempt from making full AASB S2 disclosures. Instead, its climate statement consists of:A formal statement asserting that the entity has no material financial risks or opportunities relating to climate; andA statement explaining the detailed reasoning and methods used to reach that determination.This assessment must follow AASB S2 materiality thresholds (where information is material if omitting, misstating, or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence primary users' decisions). The entity must maintain robust sustainability records to substantiate its determination.Mandatory Scenario Analysis StandardsEntities must evaluate their climate resilience using scenario analysis commensurate with their size and operational complexity (AASB S2 paragraph 22). Under section 296D(2B), entities must utilize a minimum of two climate scenarios anchored to global average temperature benchmarks referenced in section 3(a) of the Climate Change Act 2022:Lower Global Warming Scenario: Global temperature rise limited to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels (contemplating rapid global decarbonisation and high transition risks).Higher Global Warming Scenario: Global temperature rise well exceeding 2.0°C above pre-industrial levels (contemplating severe physical impacts). Note: Paragraph 2.17 of the Revised Supplementary Explanatory Memorandum to the FMI Bill clarifies that a scenario reflecting a 2.5°C or higher increase meets the requirement of well exceeding 2.0°C.Scope 3 Greenhouse Gas Emissions ReportingEntities must disclose absolute gross Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions (indirect value chain emissions, both upstream and downstream) under AASB S2 paragraph 29(a).Financed Emissions: Entities engaged in commercial banking, asset management, or insurance activities must disclose financed emissions (Scope 3, Category 15) in accordance with paragraphs B58–B63 of Appendix B of AASB S2.Data Sources and Estimation: Entities may utilize primary data (e.g., direct supplier utility records) or secondary data (e.g., industry averages, published databases) and are explicitly permitted to use estimation techniques. AASB S2 requires entities to use "all reasonable and supportable information available without undue cost or effort."First-Year Transition Relief: Entities receive mandatory transition relief exempting them from disclosing Scope 3 emissions and comparative information during their first annual reporting period under AASB S2 (Appendix C, paragraphs C3–C4).Cross-Referencing, Presentation, and LabellingCross-Referencing: A sustainability report may cross-reference disclosures contained in another report prepared by the same reporting entity (e.g., an Operating and Financial Review), provided it meets Appendix D (paragraphs 63, B45–B47) of AASB S2. Cross-referencing to reports prepared by third parties is strictly prohibited. ASIC strongly encourages lodging cross-referenced reports simultaneously with the sustainability report.Clear Identification: Disclosures required under Chapter 2M and AASB S2 must be clearly identifiable and not obscured by voluntary sustainability content (AASB S2 paragraph 62). Entities including broader voluntary content (e.g., under voluntary standard AASB S1) should include a prominent index table referencing specific paragraph numbers.Simultaneous Lodgement: The sustainability report must cover the exact same reporting period and be lodged simultaneously with the annual financial report (AASB S2 paragraph 64).5. Governance, Director Duties, and Record-Keeping ObligationsSustainability Records (Sections 285 & 286A)Reporting entities must maintain written "sustainability records" that explain the methods, assumptions, inputs, working papers, and evidence used to compile the climate statements.Retention Period: Records must be retained for seven years after the completion of the report.Strict Liability: Failure to keep adequate sustainability records is a strict liability offence (section 286A(4)).Location and Format: Electronic records must be convertible into hard copy within a reasonable timeframe. If records are kept outside Australia, sufficient written summary information must be maintained in Australia to enable report preparation (section 289A).ASIC Access: Records must be produced upon ASIC's request under compulsory information-gathering powers. Failure to produce supporting records may lead ASIC to treat disclosures as unsubstantiated.Director Duties and Oversight ExpectationsDirectors (of companies, responsible entities, RSE licensees, and retail CCIVs) must apply care and diligence (e.g., section 180(1) Corporations Act; section 52A(2)(b) SIS Act) to sustainability reporting. Directors are expected to:Understand the entity's sustainability reporting obligations and climate risk exposures;Establish robust internal controls, systems, data pipelines, and oversight procedures;Exercise critical inquiry regarding assumptions, methodologies, and potential omissions; andReview advice provided by internal/external experts in good faith, making an independent assessment using their own skills and judgment (section 189(b)).Phased Directors' Declaration RequirementsSection 296A(6) establishes a two-tiered transitional structure for the directors' declaration:2025 – 2027 Financial Years                 2028 Financial Years Onwards

Temporary Modified Liability Regime (Part 2M.3 / Section 1707D)To provide reporting entities with a transition period while data capabilities mature, Parliament established temporary "modified liability settings." During specified immunity windows, no legal action other than criminal proceedings or regulatory action brought by ASIC may be initiated against any person (including directors and auditors) regarding "protected statements."Categories of Protected StatementsProtected Statement CategoryStatutory Scope and CriteriaModified Liability Protection WindowForward-Looking Climate InformationStatements relating to climate that are about the future, made in a sustainability report or auditor's report for complying with sustainability or auditing standards.Reports prepared for financial years commencing between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2025.Scope 3 Emissions, Scenarios, and PlansStatements made in a sustainability report or auditor's report regarding:<br>1. Scope 3 GHG emissions (including financed emissions);<br>2. Climate scenario analysis; or<br>3. Climate transition plans.Reports prepared for financial years commencing between 1 January 2025 and 31 December 2027.Application Outside the Sustainability ReportModified liability protection extends to statements made outside the sustainability report only if the statement is required to be disclosed under a Commonwealth law (e.g., continuous disclosure under sections 674/675, OFR under section 299A, prospectuses under section 710, PDSs under section 1013D/E, or ASIC section 296E directions) and is:Identical to the protected statement in the sustainability report; orDiffers only to the extent that it updates or corrects the protected statement.Exclusions from Liability Protection: Immunity does not apply to voluntary public disclosures (e.g., investor presentation slides, voluntary marketing collateral), statements incorporated by cross-reference into the sustainability report from external reports, or expanded non-statutory summaries.7. Sustainability Disclosures Across Other Corporate DocumentsASIC encourages entities to adopt the defined terms in Appendix A of AASB S1/AASB S2 and the qualitative presentation principles in Appendix D (neutrality, balanced risk/opportunity framing, clear identification of estimates) whenever making climate disclosures across broader corporate reporting channels.Operating and Financial Review (OFR - Section 299A)Listed entities must include climate-related financial information in their OFR if members reasonably require that information to make an informed assessment of operations, financial position, business strategies, and prospects. The OFR serves to frame specific climate disclosures within the entity's broader overarching strategic context.Capital Raising Documents (Chapter 6D Prospectuses)Section 710 Prospectuses: Must disclose material climate risks and strategies where required for investors and advisers to assess the issuer's assets, liabilities, financial position, performance, and prospects.Transaction-Specific Prospectuses (Section 713): Must explicitly inform investors of their right to obtain a copy of the entity's most recently lodged sustainability report, or attach a copy directly.Offer Information Statements (OIS - Section 715): Must attach a copy of the most recently prepared, audited sustainability report.Product Disclosure Statements (PDS - Part 7.9)General PDS Duties (Sections 1013D & 1013E): Must include climate information if it represents a significant risk, benefit, or product feature influencing a retail client's decision.Investment Products (Section 1013D(1)(l) & Regulation 7.9.14C): PDS disclosures for financial products with an investment component taking into account climate considerations must clearly specify the applicable standards, methodologies, weighting, and extent of integration, following ASIC Regulatory Guide 65 guidelines.Superannuation Products (Section 1017C(3AA)): RSE issuers must provide a copy of the sustainability report and auditor's report upon written request from any "concerned person" (member or beneficiary).8. ASIC Directions Power (Section 296E)If ASIC considers that a statement in a sustainability report is incorrect, incomplete, or misleading, ASIC may issue a written direction ordering the entity to:Confirm the statement is correct or complete;Explain or substantiate the statement;Correct, complete, or amend the statement; and/orPublish or distribute the corrected statement to specified market participants.Procedural Protections: Before ordering an entity to correct, amend, or publish a statement, ASIC must grant the entity an opportunity to attend a private administrative hearing and submit evidence. Non-compliance with an issued ASIC direction is a strict liability offence (section 296E(8)). ASIC publishes all direction notices, variations, or revocations on its website.Relief Powers and Exemption Preconditions (Part 2M.6)Under section 340 (individual relief) and section 341 (class relief), ASIC may grant prospective relief exempting entities from sustainability reporting or audit duties.Jurisdictional Thresholds (Section 342(1)): ASIC can only grant relief if satisfied that compliance would:Make the report misleading (section 342(1)(a));Be inappropriate in the circumstances (section 342(1)(b) — requiring an anomaly in the law or unintended legislative outcomes); orImpose unreasonable burdens (section 342(1)(c) — where administrative costs are proven to be grossly disproportionate to user disclosure benefits).Key Relief Principles:Ordinary Compliance Costs: Ordinary administrative expenses, expert consulting fees, audit costs, or private entity status do not constitute an unreasonable burden.Prospective Filing: Applications must be lodged via the ASIC Regulatory Portal before the statutory filing deadline. ASIC cannot grant retrospective relief after a deadline has passed.Duration: Individual relief granted during early transition phases will generally be short-term, typically limited to a single 12-month reporting period.Extensions of Time: Lodgement extensions are granted only in rare, extreme circumstances. Auditor delays, staff absences, or pending corporate transactions do not justify an extension.Auditing and Assurance TimelineSustainability reports must be audited under Part 2M.3 Division 3 (section 301A). Auditing requirements are subject to a progressive phase-in timeline established by the Australian Auditing and Assurance Standards Board (AUASB):1 January 2025 to 30 June 2030: Review or audit assurance required in accordance with the AUASB phased timetable.1 July 2030 Onwards: Full mandatory statutory audit required for all reporting entities.Key Regulatory Terms ReferenceTermStatutory Definition and Source ContextAASB S2AASB S2 Climate-related disclosures — mandatory sustainability standard made under section 336A of the Corporations Act (based on IFRS S2).Climate ResilienceThe capacity of an entity to adjust to climate-related changes, developments, or uncertainties, incorporating both strategic and operational resilience.Financed EmissionsGross greenhouse gas emissions of investees or counterparties attributed to loans and investments made by an entity (Scope 3, Category 15).Net GHG TargetAn entity's targeted gross greenhouse gas emissions minus planned offsetting efforts (such as carbon credit retirements).Protected StatementStatements covered by temporary statutory liability protection under section 1707D, restricting legal proceedings to criminal or ASIC actions.RSE LicenseeEntity holding a registrable superannuation entity licence under section 10 of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993.Sustainability RecordsDocuments and working papers explaining the methods, assumptions, inputs, and evidence supporting a sustainability report (section 9).
